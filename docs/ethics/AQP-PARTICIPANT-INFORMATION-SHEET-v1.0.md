## Why am I being invited?

We are testing two ways to set up an online questionnaire study. We are testing
the software, not you.

You may take part if you are 18 or over, can use a desktop or laptop browser,
have not developed AQP, and have set up no more than two online questionnaire
studies before.

## Do I have to take part?

No. Taking part is your choice. You may ask questions before deciding. You may
take a break, skip an optional feedback question, or stop at any time without
giving a reason or losing any benefit.

## What will I do?

The remote session will take up to 75 minutes.

1. Read this sheet and complete the consent form.
2. Tell us about your previous survey-platform experience and your browser.
3. Set up the same short SUS questionnaire in AQP and in a prepared UCL
   Qualtrics template. Some people use AQP first and some use Qualtrics first.
4. After each setup, give a 1–7 ease rating. At the end, answer a short standard
   usability questionnaire for each system and three short feedback questions.
5. Check two setup materials for any difference you would resolve before
   releasing a participant link.

Use only the synthetic files and details supplied by the research team. Do not
enter real participant data.

## Is anything intentionally hidden?

One checking task may contain a small intentional difference. We do not tell you
its location in advance because noticing it is part of the task. We will explain
the difference immediately afterwards. It is not a test of you and it is not
your mistake.

## Will the session be recorded?

Yes. Screen and session-audio recording is required for this study because it is
needed to measure time, assists and errors and to let a second researcher check the
coding. You do not have to agree. If you do not agree to recording, you will not
take part in this recorded study, and there is no penalty. We do not intend to
record your camera.

Because the session runs on your own computer, a recording could accidentally
show an account name, browser tab, notification, file path or other identifying
detail. Before recording, please close unrelated windows and mute notifications.
Do not open private files or real participant data. You may ask to pause the task
and recording, or stop the session, at any time. A paused section is not used as
task-performance data.

## What data will be collected?

- your study code and relevant survey-platform experience;
- device, operating system and browser;
- task outcome, active time, assists, errors and whether you found the planned
  difference;
- 1–7 ease ratings, SUS answers and short feedback;
- the study-window recording and session audio;
- a separate consent and contact record.

We do not ask for a diagnosis, health information or special-category personal
data.

## What are the risks and benefits?

An unfamiliar setup task may feel tiring or frustrating. You may take a break or
stop. The planned difference may briefly make you unsure; we explain it straight
after the task. The main privacy risk is accidental identifying material in the
recording. The steps above reduce this risk but cannot remove it completely.

There may be no direct benefit to you. Your feedback may help improve how online
questionnaire studies are prepared and checked.

## Will I be paid?

No payment is planned for this study. Taking part should not create any cost for
you.

## How will my data be protected?

We use a study code in task data. Contact and consent records are kept separately.
Recordings are moved immediately to a restricted UCL Research Data Storage
Service project folder. After the transfer is checked, the temporary local copy
is securely removed. Recordings are not emailed or uploaded to GitHub or personal
cloud storage.

Access is limited to Yurui Wang, Dr Mark Colley and one named UCL second coder.
The second coder independently checks at least two sessions. Reports use
de-identified results and short anonymous quotations.

Raw screen and audio recordings will be securely deleted no later than
30 November 2026, after coding and checking are complete. The deletion date will
be recorded. Consent records and de-identified research data are kept separately
for the periods required by UCL policy.

## Can I withdraw my data?

You may stop during the session. You may also ask us to remove your identifiable
and coded data within seven calendar days after your session by contacting
Dr Mark Colley and giving your study code. After data have been irreversibly
anonymised and combined, it may no longer be possible to remove one person's
data.

## What will happen to the results?

The results may appear in a dissertation, research paper, presentation or
teaching material. You will not be named. This small study is formative; it does
not prove that either platform is easier for all researchers.

## Privacy, questions and complaints

UCL is the data controller. The lawful basis is performance of a task in the
public interest. No special-category data is requested. Read the
[UCL General Research Participant Privacy Notice](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/legal-services/privacy/ucl-general-research-participant-privacy-notice).

For study questions or concerns, contact Dr Mark Colley at m.colley@ucl.ac.uk.
For data-protection questions, contact data-protection@ucl.ac.uk. If a concern is
not resolved, contact the UCL Research Ethics Service at ethics@ucl.ac.uk.

Thank you for reading this sheet.
