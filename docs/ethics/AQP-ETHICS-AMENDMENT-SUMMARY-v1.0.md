## Why the change is needed

The earlier self-administered researcher task relied on unobserved self-report.
It could not reliably measure task success, time, assistance or errors. The
revised study uses observable task outcomes, a fixed script and a pre-specified
analysis plan. The dissertation remains complete without this study: quantified
technical evaluation and a manual assistive-technology audit form the core
evaluation. Approved sessions, if completed in time, are additional formative
evidence only. Otherwise the fully specified protocol is reported as planned
work and the researcher-usability claim remains not evidenced.

## Participants

- 6–8 adults aged 18 or over.
- Able to use a desktop or laptop browser and understand the study language.
- No prior development work on AQP.
- Novice or occasional questionnaire researchers: no more than two previously
  configured online questionnaire studies.
- Willing to take part in an observed remote session and consent to recording of
  the study window and session audio.

No diagnosis, health information or special-category personal data is requested.
Routine experience with Qualtrics or another survey platform is recorded because
it is a relevant task characteristic.

## What changes in the method

Each session lasts up to 75 minutes and uses synthetic questionnaire data only.
Participants complete the same SUS setup task in two whole-tool conditions:

1. AQP, starting from the researcher landing page with built-in SUS available.
2. UCL Qualtrics, starting from a fresh copy of the frozen
   `AQP-SUS-COMPARISON-v1` template. The template already contains the ten SUS
   items, required settings, one-question-per-page presentation and verified
   Brooke score calculation.

The required end state is frozen in the protocol. Condition order is
counterbalanced. The observer uses a fixed neutral script and does not coach.
The study records task success, active time, assists, observable errors, the
Single Ease Question, SUS for each system and three short open questions.

After both conditions, participants check a source sheet against an imported
definition containing one mild, intentionally planted label difference. The
information sheet tells participants that one task may contain a small
intentional difference but does not reveal its location. The observer explains
the exact difference immediately after the task and confirms that it was not the
participant's mistake.

## Recording and incidental identifying material

With separate active consent, the study window and session audio are recorded
only to code task time, assists and errors. A recording made on a participant's
own computer may accidentally capture identifying material such as an account
name, browser tabs, notifications, file paths or voice. Before recording, the
participant is asked to close unrelated windows and mute notifications. They may
pause or stop recording at any time, but the risk cannot be eliminated entirely.

The study does not intentionally record the participant's camera. The
participant is asked not to open real participant data or unrelated private
material during the session.

## Data handling, access and destruction

- Assign a study code. Keep contact and consent records separate from task data.
- Transfer each recording immediately to an access-controlled UCL Research Data
  Storage Service (RDSS) project folder registered by the supervisor. Verify the
  transfer, then securely remove the temporary local copy.
- Do not email recordings or upload them to GitHub, personal cloud storage or an
  open repository.
- Limit access to Yurui Wang, Dr Mark Colley and one named UCL second coder listed
  in the approved amendment. The second coder independently codes at least two
  sessions and receives only the minimum files needed.
- Delete raw screen/audio recordings no later than **30 November 2026**, after
  primary coding and the pre-specified agreement check are complete. Record the
  deletion date.
- Retain consent records separately for the period required by the UCL Records
  Retention Schedule. Retain only de-identified coded research data for the
  approved research-retention period.
- A participant may request removal of their identifiable and coded data using
  their study code until seven calendar days after their session. After
  irreversible anonymisation and aggregation, individual removal may no longer
  be possible.

## Analysis and safeguards

Report per-participant data and descriptive summaries only: counts and
denominators, median, minimum and maximum. Do not use significance tests or claim
population effects at n=6–8. Define task success, assist and error before the
first session. Preregister the protocol, frozen systems, counterbalancing,
analysis and second-coder rule. A second person independently codes at least two
recordings and pre-consensus agreement is reported.

The participant may take a break or stop without penalty. No payment is planned
under this amendment. Adding payment, changing the recording route, recruiting a
different participant group or adding a disabled-respondent study requires prior
ethics confirmation and updated participant materials.
