# Project Risk Assessment

**Project:** Accessible Questionnaire Platform — observed researcher study
**Version:** 1.0, 9 August 2026
**Student researcher:** Yurui Wang
**Principal investigator/supervisor:** Dr Mark Colley
**Setting:** One-to-one observed remote sessions on participants' own computers
**Participants:** 6–8 adults acting as novice questionnaire researchers

This assessment covers only the optional observed researcher study. The core
technical evaluation and the researcher's manual assistive-technology audit do
not collect participant data. The withdrawn disabled-respondent comparison is
outside this amendment.

## Overall decision

The study is low risk after the controls below. Do not recruit, consent or record
any participant until the amendment is approved in writing, the independent
second coder is named and the restricted UCL storage location is active.

| Hazard | Who may be affected | Initial risk | Controls | Residual risk and action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Accidental identity, account name, tabs, notifications or file paths in a screen recording | Participant | Medium | Explain the risk before consent; record the study window rather than the full desktop where the meeting tool permits; ask the participant to close unrelated windows and mute notifications; use only synthetic files; allow pause/stop; review only for coding. | Low. If unrelated sensitive material appears, stop, minimise further access and securely delete or redact the affected segment where feasible. Record the incident and tell the supervisor. |
| Unauthorised access, loss or incorrect transfer of recordings | Participant and research team | Medium | Transfer immediately to an access-controlled UCL RDSS project folder; verify the transfer; securely remove the temporary local copy; never email or upload recordings to GitHub/personal cloud; separate consent/contact records from coded data; restrict access to the student, supervisor and named second coder. | Low. Report any suspected breach through UCL procedures and suspend coding until advised. |
| Keeping identifiable recordings longer than needed | Participant | Medium | Delete raw screen/audio recordings no later than 30 November 2026 after coding and agreement checks; record the deletion date; retain only de-identified coded data for the approved period. | Low. Supervisor checks the deletion log. |
| Mild deception in the planted-difference task causes uncertainty or embarrassment | Participant | Low | Information sheet says one task may contain a small intentional difference; observer says the tool, not the participant, is being tested; debrief immediately with the exact difference and state that it was not the participant's mistake; permit withdrawal. | Low. Stop and debrief early if distress is visible or reported. |
| Frustration, fatigue or performance anxiety during unfamiliar setup tasks | Participant | Low | Limit the session to 75 minutes and each setup condition to 20 active minutes; use a fixed neutral script; allow breaks and stopping; do not grade the participant; collect only short questionnaires and open comments. | Low. Stop the session if the participant requests it or shows marked distress. |
| Observer coaching or inconsistent judgement biases task success, assists or errors | Research quality and participant | Medium | Freeze the task, start/end state, success/assist/error definitions and permitted prompts; counterbalance condition order; record with consent; independently code at least two sessions; report pre-consensus agreement and deviations. | Low for a formative study. Do not make population claims or run significance tests. |
| Use of a participant's live Qualtrics workspace reveals real research data | Participant or third parties | Medium | Use a fresh copy of the study-owned UCL Qualtrics template and synthetic task details only; tell participants not to open real surveys or participant data; stop recording if unrelated data appear. | Low. Delete/redact accidental capture and report if personal data were exposed. |
| Participant cannot exercise withdrawal because contact and task data are mixed | Participant | Low | Use a study code; store the code key and consent/contact data separately; accept withdrawal requests for seven calendar days after the session; document when data become irreversibly anonymised. | Low. Confirm withdrawal and deletion in writing. |
| Remote session interruption, connection failure or loss of a partial recording | Participant and research quality | Low | Pause the timer for researcher-owned outages; permit rescheduling; do not treat technical failure as participant error; verify files before deleting the temporary copy; document protocol deviations. | Low. Exclude only under pre-specified rules and report the case. |

## Data-protection controls

- The student researcher completes required UCL data-protection training before
  handling recordings.
- No special-category personal data is requested.
- UCL is the data controller; research uses the public-task lawful basis.
- The participant receives the UCL General Research Participant Privacy Notice.
- Consent records are retained separately under the UCL Records Retention
  Schedule. De-identified research data follow the approved research-retention
  period.
- Raw recordings are not open data and will not be published.

## Researcher safety

Sessions are remote and concern non-sensitive software tasks. No in-person,
off-site or lone visit is required. The researcher may stop a session, report an
incident to the supervisor and use UCL wellbeing or safety procedures if needed.
